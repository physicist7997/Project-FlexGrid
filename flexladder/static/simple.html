2The crisis

It's a 104°F September evening in the San Fernando Valley. At 6:00 PM a substation is overloading. The utility calls: cut MW within 30 minutes and hold it for , or they start switching whole neighborhoods off.

3What FlexLadder did, minute by minute

Least disruptive first. Each step only uses devices whose owners signed up, that are actually running right now, and only within the owner's limits.

    ✅ FlexLadder: everyone gives a little

    homes completely dark

    🧊 Fridges, lights and Wi-Fi are never touched
    ⚕ Medical homes: equipment and cooling always protected
    🚗 Every EV is still charged by its owner's morning time
    🔋 Batteries never go below the owner's backup reserve

    5Over the next hours, honestly

    FlexLadder re-plans every 15 minutes. Thermostat savings fade as homes warm up, and water heaters and pool pumps can only wait so long, so it reports what it can really deliver.

    Throw a curveball

    Something breaks mid-event. FlexLadder re-plans instantly with what's still working.

    ★On an ordinary Tuesday

    The same system runs a few times a month in summer to trim expensive peak hours. Homeowners earn bill credits, the utility avoids buying costly peak power, and the whole network is already set up and tested when a real emergency hits.
